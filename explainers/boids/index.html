Boids: the pigeons of Washington Square

No pigeon is in charge. Each one just watches the few birds next to it, and somehow a flock appears.

In 1987 Craig Reynolds showed you could fake a convincing flock with three local rules. Each "boid" looks only at neighbours within a small radius and nudges its own heading:

  1. Separation: don't crowd. Steer away from birds that are too close.
  2. Alignment: go with the flow. Turn toward the average heading of your neighbours.
  3. Cohesion: stick together. Drift toward the centre of your neighbours.

That's the whole algorithm. Turn the rules up and down below and watch what changes. Press and drag on the park to fly the hawk; every pigeon near it panics and flees.

What to try

  • Set cohesion to 0. Pigeons still line up, but loose groups never merge.
  • Set separation to 0. The flock collapses into tight clumps.
  • Set alignment to 0. Birds bunch together but mill about with no shared direction.
  • Tick "Show what one pigeon sees". The dashed circle is all it knows about; the coloured arrows are its three rules pulling at it.
